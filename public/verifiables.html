What belongs in this line

Three conditions. All three, or it isn't a Verifiable.

01

You must trust it. Using it means granting a privilege — power, data, containment, custody.

02

The abuse is invisible. If the object betrayed that privilege, normal use would never show it.

03

A physical fact settles it. A property that either exists or doesn't, checkable by a non-expert with a simple instrument.

Condition three is the filter, and it excludes most of the security market. "Trust our firmware" is not checkable. "There is no conductor here" is.

A Verifiable makes a claim about itself. Products that make claims about the world — "there is nothing hidden in this room" — are unprovable negatives, and they don't belong here regardless of how large that market is.

The assurance ladder

There is no ladder here.

You don't need one. Everywhere else in this company there's a ladder — how strong is the backing, how much do you still have to trust us. This line has no rungs, because there is nothing to grade.

A Verifiable holds no key and signs nothing. It has no identity of its own, at any tier. What it carries is a record of how it was made — not a claim about what it has done since. That isn't a limitation we're working around; it's the entire honesty of the line, and it's what lets proof by inspection be literally true.

Hold it up to the light. That's the whole mechanism.

Beyond the line

Where Verifiables end

When you need a record that a specific human approved a specific thing at a specific moment, inspection isn't enough — you need a device that signs.

And when a stranger has to check that record years later, you need a rail that outlives the transaction.

Verify it yourself

Don't trust it. Check it.

See it

Look through the resin. Count the parts. The source connector is a six-pin power-only part — the data pins aren't unconnected, they're not present in the connector body. On the board they're marked NC.

Test it

Plug a computer into the input and your phone into the output. Your phone charges. It never appears on that computer — no notification, nothing in Device Manager. It can't enumerate, because enumeration needs a data path.

Thirty seconds, and it requires believing nothing we've said.

Look it up

Every unit carries a serial and a short code sealed inside the resin. Enter it at verifyknot.io and the verdict is computed in your browser, from the record itself. Our server isn't asked for its opinion.

Every claim is on the object

We can revise this page. We can't revise cast resin. So the claims live on the board too, in silkscreen:

DATA LINES PHYSICALLY ABSENT — NO USB ENUMERATION
SAFE POWER ONLY · DATA ABSENT
Your charger can't spy through this.
When physics is policy, trust is optional.

What these do not do

Straight talk, before you buy rather than after.

A Verifiable signs nothing. It has no key and no microcontroller. It cannot record, measure, or attest to anything that happens while you use it. If you need that, you need an attestation device, and we'll tell you so.

Its record is our signature, not an independent one. The manufacturing record on the rail is ZKNOT's signed statement that we built this specific unit. It proves the record hasn't changed since we made it. It does not prove we're honest — nothing signed by us ever could. What it does is make our claim specific, dated, and checkable, so a lie would be a fixed target instead of a vague impression.

PowerVerify protects one path. It stops data over that cable. It says nothing about any other way into your device.

A seal shows tampering. It doesn't prevent it. Tamper-evident is the ceiling, and we won't say tamper-proof.
