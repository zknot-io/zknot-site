Where a stranger — present nowhere, trusting no one — turns a code into an answer. The verdict is computed in your browser, not on our server. And we publish which rungs of the ladder we haven't built.

What belongs here

Three conditions.

01

It preserves a claim. Something signed, hashed, or registered is kept in a form that can be examined again later.

02

Someone else can examine it. Not just us, and not just the person who created it.

03

Two people checking it independently get the same answer.

The rails are infrastructure, not a product line. Verification is free and has no login. The chain is a property of records, not something you buy. One rail component is a paid product — HashStamp — and it stamps documents, not hardware.

The independence ladder

How much do you still need us?

Every verification claim sits somewhere on this ladder. Most companies never tell you where. Here is ours, including the rungs we haven't built.

RUNG 1You take our word for it

The record says we registered something. If we're dishonest or gone, the claim is worth nothing. This is where a seal registration sits.

RUNG 2You need us online

The record is real and checkable, but you have to ask our API for it. If we're offline, you can't look it up.

RUNG 3You need our math, not our wordYou are here

This is where verification actually happens. Your browser re-runs the signature check itself. We are not consulted for the verdict — and the page will tell you the signature failed even when our own server thinks the record is fine.

RUNG 4You need nothing from usWe have not built this

Checking a record offline, from a file you hold, with no network and no ZKNOT. This is the rung that would make a record outlive the company. It does not exist yet. There is no export, no bundle, no offline verifier.

RUNG 5Someone outside vouches for the timeWe have not built this

An independent timestamp authority confirming when, so the time is not our word. We have not built this either, on any product.

We are on rung 3. Publishing that is the point. Any vendor can say "verified." Very few will tell you which rung their answer sits on, or which of their claims stop working the day they go out of business.

What a stranger can establish

Six questions

A verification rail is only as good as its answer to each one — and only as honest as its willingness to say which ones it does not answer.

QuestionWhat we can show you
1Did the signature verify?Yes — and your browser does the checking. We re-verify server-side on every request too, but the verdict you see is computed on your machine.
2Is the signing key one ZKNOT vouches for?Yes or no, stated plainly. Records signed by keys we don't vouch for are accepted into the chain and shown as unvouched — we don't hide them and we don't pretend they're evidence.
3Has the record been altered?The record is hash-linked to the ones before it. Change a signed field and the linkage breaks for everyone checking, permanently.
4Who is asserting this — a device or a registry?Stated on every record, in words, with what it does not prove sitting next to what it does.
5When did it happen, and on whose word?On ours. The time on every ZKNOT record is supplied by the service that wrote it. It is not confirmed by any outside authority.
6What exactly did the signature cover?Stated per record. On older records the honest answer is "the fingerprint, and nothing else" — and that is what the record says.

The chain itself

THE LEDGER

A hash-linked, append-only ledger. Not a blockchain — there is no network, no consensus, no token, and no mining.

It is a chain of hashes in a database, and the only thing it is good for is making alteration detectable. That is also the only thing we claim it does.

What we don't claim

The list nobody else publishes.

We don't anchor time to anyone outside ZKNOT. Every timestamp on every ZKNOT record — including paid HashStamp records — is supplied by our service. If you need a time an adversary can't argue with, we are not that today, and we would rather say so than let you find out in a dispute.

You can't verify offline yet. Checking a record requires our API to answer. A record you can check with no network and no ZKNOT is the thing we most want to ship and have not shipped.

We don't publish our registry key, so "is this key ours?" is our word. Your browser genuinely re-checks the signature — that part you can watch happen. Whether the key belongs to a device we vouch for is reported by us, and there is currently nowhere independent for you to check it. Until we publish that key, treat key identity as our assertion.

Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. Altering a signed field is detectable by anyone checking. It is not prevented.

Not a blockchain. No network, no consensus, no token.

A signature proves less than people assume. It shows a specific key signed specific bytes. It does not show who held the key, that a person was present, that anyone approved anything, or that the contents are true.

Where this stands

Live, and honest about the rest.

Verification is live and free at verifyknot.io. HashStamp is live and takes payment. Offline verification and independent time anchoring are the next two things we intend to build, and neither exists today.
