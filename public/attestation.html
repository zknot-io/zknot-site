ZKNOT Attestation Devices

The device
signs it. Not us.

Hardware that turns a physical act — a specific human pressing, at a moment, over a specific thing — into a record a stranger can check. Four rungs, and they differ by threat model, not by being "more secure."

Where this line stands

None of these ship today.

We would rather you learn that here than after a checkout. Every device below exists as hardware — boards are built, silicon is proven — and not one of them can yet be provisioned onto the verification rail, which is what makes an attestation device worth owning.

Each product says exactly what is blocking it. Some are hardware, most are not. When one clears, it gets a price and a buy button on this page and not before.

If you want the thing that works today, it's the Verifiables line — no keys, no provisioning, nothing to wait for.

What belongs in this line

Three conditions.

01

It holds a key. A secret in hardware that the article itself can sign with.

02

It signs something specific. A defined payload — an identity, a presence event, a measurement — not a general assertion.

03

The claim survives us. A third party can re-verify the signature later without ZKNOT's participation or continued existence.

A passive article can't join this line no matter how strong its physical claim is. No key, no signature. That's why PowerVerify and TamperVerify are Verifiables — a separation of construction, not a ranking.

The honesty ladder

What actually backs the claim

Three levels of backing. The difference isn't how good the cryptography is — it's who vouches for the key that did the signing.

LEVEL 1The submitter's own wordEvery record today

A signature exists and verifies against the key carried in the record. Nothing establishes whose key it is. The owner is whoever the record claims.

LEVEL 2Our registry vouches

ZKNOT's own key signed the registration. Better than nothing and still our word — a registry key is not a device key, and we say which one signed.

LEVEL 3A key held in hardware, that we vouch forTarget

The signature came from a key generated inside a secure element that has never left it, and that key is on file with us. This is what the devices below are built for.

Every ZKNOT record in existence today sits at level 1. Not one has reached level 3. The hardware is capable of it; the provisioning that would make it true has not shipped. Publishing that is the point — the bottom rung existing on purpose is what would make the top rung believable.

The four rungs

Different threats, not different grades

Selling these as good / better / best would misprice both ends. Each defeats something the one below it doesn't.

SelfKnot

Open on-ramp · defeats casual forgery

The open tier. A seeed Xiao board plus a secure element holding a key generated on-chip that cannot be extracted in software.

The firmware is open and user-replaceable by design — and that's the honest claim: trust comes from the ledger-enrolled key, not from firmware you could replace yourself.

  • Xiao (seeed) + ATECC608B secure element
  • P-256 keypair generated on-chip, non-extractable
  • Potted · $59
  • No confirm button — it makes no human-presence claim

Ostensor

$129
Flagship · defeats deception at signing time

Latin, one who shows. The only device in the line that addresses the hardest problem: what you were shown is what actually got signed.

A secure domain owns both the display and the key, so a compromised application can't show you one thing and sign another. This mechanism is proven in silicon on the bench — and it is the only device here that carries it.

  • STM32U585 + Infineon OPTIGA Trust M
  • Show-then-confirm: the display and the key share one secure domain
  • One physical press permits at most one signature
Blocked: the security hardening that makes the above enforceable is not yet switched on in shipped units. Proven on the bench, not in a product.

What these do not do

Stated before you wait for one.

A signature proves less than people assume. It shows a specific key signed specific bytes. On its own it does not show who held the key, that a person was present, that anyone approved anything, or that the contents are true. Each rung above adds one of those, and we say which.

Only Ostensor addresses show-then-confirm. SelfKnot has no confirm button. Vitni's current revision supports no human-presence claim. If someone tells you a device proves a human approved something, ask which part of the hardware enforces that — and we'll answer the same question about ours.

Every record today sits at the bottom of the ladder. Not one ZKNOT record has reached level 3. The devices are built for it; the provisioning is not shipped.

The time on a record is ours. No ZKNOT record's timestamp is confirmed by an outside authority. That's a property of the rails, and it applies here too.

Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof. Nothing here prevents a determined attacker with physical possession — it makes the attempt detectable.

Want to be told when one of these actually ships? Write to us and say which. We'll tell you what cleared and what's still open, in the same words we use here.

Where this sits
